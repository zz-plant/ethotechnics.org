# Homepage revamp

The owner authorizes a substantial redesign and overrides maintenance for this homepage task.
The accepted opening is “Who keeps the system working—and can they make it change?”

## Intent

Explain the framework's particular connection between hidden human compensation, correction of
underlying rules, and preservation of corrective capacity. Make the research and instruments
inspectable without overstating originality, adoption, or validation.

## Approaches

An expanded Robodebt story would preserve the current narrative but still introduce the project
through a single failure. A tool-first dashboard would privilege self-reported scores over the
argument. The selected approach is an editorial research front page: a large thesis, a compact
account of the contribution, evidence, and explicit routes into the work.

## Composition

Use a bold serif masthead, a deep ink evidence section, warm paper, ruled editorial sections,
and ruled reading lists. Preserve the shared site chrome. A small typographic comparison explains
the difference between reported success and the human work it omits; it uses no invented data.

Reading order: thesis and definition; three linked concerns; the Robodebt example and casebook;
the distinct purpose of research, standards, and checks; existing self-test; reader routes; author
and correction information. Preserve `#record`, `#mechanism`, and `#self-test` destinations.

## Boundaries

No tool retirement, schema changes, global rebrand, new dependencies, or external service. Existing
case facts remain sourced through the casebook. The self-test stays explicitly self-reported.
The homepage CSS is loaded only by this route, and new display sizes live in theme tokens.

## Verification

Run the repository check and production build. Inspect desktop and 320px mobile in light and dark
themes, navigation anchors, self-test completion/share restoration, reduced motion, and no-JS
reading. Check internal destinations, browser errors, horizontal overflow, and accessibility.
