title: "What is Stoppability?"
description: "Stoppability explains the right to halt automated processes and protect people from forced tunnels."
permalink: "/explainers/stoppability"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "stoppability"
definition:
  - "Stoppability is the requirement that people can halt an automated process instantly without losing their place or facing penalties. It centers on visible stop controls, state preservation, and the ability to walk away without retribution."
  - "Teams use stoppability to prevent forced tunnels and to prove that systems respect human time and agency."
example: "A loan application flow includes a persistent “Stop and resume later” control that saves progress, issues a receipt, and confirms the application will not be penalized for pausing."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Stop-control criteria"
    detail: "Require always-visible halt controls, no-penalty pause paths, and resumable state snapshots across every critical journey."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Flows claim stoppability but invalidate progress after pause; test resume behavior under network loss and session expiry."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Share stop-request volumes, successful resume rates, and audit logs proving no punitive flags were attached after halts."
