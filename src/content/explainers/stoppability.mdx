---
title: "What is stoppability?"
description: "Stoppability is the ability to halt an automated process: for the person inside it, without penalty; for the institution, by a named person, in a set time."
permalink: "/explainers/stoppability"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "stoppability"
definition:
  - "Stoppability has two sides. A person inside an automated process can leave it without losing their place or being penalized for leaving; STD-01 Article I makes that a right. And a named person in the institution can halt the system itself within a set time, on the record."
  - "Neither side is enough on its own. A system that can be stopped can still fix one case at a time and never change the rule that produced the harm. Stoppability contains harm while the correction happens. It is not the correction."
example: "A loan application flow has a persistent “Stop and resume later” control that saves progress and issues a receipt. Behind it, a named credit-risk lead can suspend automated declines within a day, and each suspension is logged with the evidence that prompted it."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Stop-control criteria"
    detail: "Require always-visible halt controls, no-penalty pause paths, and resumable state snapshots across every critical journey."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Flows claim stoppability but invalidate progress after pause, or the institution's halt exists only on paper: nobody is named and it has never been used. Test resume behavior under network loss and session expiry, and ask who last used the halt."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Share stop-request volumes, successful resume rates, and audit logs proving no punitive flags were attached after halts. For the institution's halt, name the person who holds it and the date it was last used."

faqs:
  - question: "How is stoppability different from cancellation?"
    answer: "Cancellation ends a process, while stoppability ensures people can pause or halt it immediately without losing state or being penalized for stopping."
  - question: "What evidence demonstrates stoppability?"
    answer: "UI recordings of the halt control, event logs showing immediate cessation, and receipts proving state preservation. For the institution's halt: the named holder and the record of its last use."
  - question: "Is stoppability the center of the framework?"
    answer: "No. The center is whether evidence that a system is wrong reaches someone who has to change it. Stoppability is how harm is contained while that happens."
