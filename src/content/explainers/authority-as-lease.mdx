title: "Authority as a lease"
description: "A grant of authority is held for a term against a renewable justification, not owned outright."
permalink: "/explainers/authority-as-lease"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "authority-lease"
definition:
  - "Treating authority as a lease means a grant expires by default, and continuing it takes fresh evidence. The delegation carries the burden of proof, and the burden grows with how long it runs and how much its decisions matter."
  - "The alternative treats authority as a title. Then silence renews it: a system that has not visibly failed keeps its scope indefinitely."
example: "A vendor scoring model holds a twelve-month grant. At month twelve the grant lapses on its own. The team must file a renewal with new calibration evidence before scoring resumes."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "A renewal date that acts on its own"
    detail: "Every grant carries a renewal date. Passing it changes the grant's state without anyone having to remember. A renewal cites new evidence, not the original approval."
  - title: "Renewal as a rubber stamp"
    detail: "Renewal exists on paper but grants are approved in batches without review, so the lease never ends. Check whether any grant has ever lapsed."
  - title: "Grants that lapsed or narrowed"
    detail: "Report the median age of live grants against their renewal interval, and how many grants lapsed or were narrowed at renewal in the last year."

faqs:
  - question: "Does a lease mean the system stops on the renewal date?"
    answer: "It means the grant stops authorizing new actions until it is renewed. Whether the system halts, or falls back to confirm mode where a person approves each action, is a choice recorded on the grant."
  - question: "Is a long lease acceptable?"
    answer: "Yes, if what is at stake and how long it runs justify the term. The term must be stated, enforced, and renewed on evidence, not on the absence of complaints."
