---
title: "Authority as a Lease"
description: "A grant of authority is held for a term against a renewable justification, not owned outright."
permalink: "/explainers/authority-as-lease"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "authority-lease"
definition:
  - "Treating authority as a lease means a grant expires by default and continuing it requires fresh evidence. The burden of proof sits with the delegation, and it rises with the duration and the consequence of what has been delegated."
  - "The alternative, treating authority as a title, makes silence the renewal mechanism: a system that has not visibly failed keeps its scope indefinitely."
example: "A vendor scoring model holds a twelve-month grant. At month twelve the grant lapses on its own, and the team must file a renewal packet with new calibration evidence before scoring resumes."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What to check"
    detail: "Every grant carries a renewal date, and passing it changes the grant state without anyone needing to remember. Renewal packets cite new evidence rather than restating the original approval."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Renewal exists on paper but is performed as a batch rubber stamp, so the lease is functionally perpetual. Look at whether any grant has ever lapsed."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Report the median age of live grants against their renewal interval, and the count of grants that lapsed or were narrowed at renewal in the last year."

faqs:
  - question: "Does a lease mean the system stops on the renewal date?"
    answer: "It means the grant stops authorizing new action until it is renewed. Whether that halts the system or drops it to a confirm-mode fallback is a design choice recorded on the grant."
  - question: "Is a long lease acceptable?"
    answer: "Yes, if the term is justified by consequence and duration. The requirement is that the term is stated, enforced, and renewed on evidence rather than on the absence of complaints."
