title: "What is an Intervention Specification?"
description: "An intervention specification replaces human in the loop with six answerable questions about who can actually change the system."
permalink: "/explainers/intervention-specification"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "intervention-specification"
definition:
  - "An intervention specification states who may intervene, on what signal, with what information, with authority over which state, on what timescale, and what happens when they disagree. It is the object every oversight clause should resolve to."
  - "Ethotechnics retires human in the loop as a control name because naming a human is compatible with that human being unable to alter anything."
example: "A payments hold queue names two on-call reviewers, the fraud-score signal that routes cases to them, the customer history they see, authority to release or freeze the account, a four-minute budget per case, and an escalation path to the duty manager when they disagree with the model."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What to check"
    detail: "The specification names a state the human can alter, not only a decision they can approve, and the disagreement path has been exercised at least once in the last quarter."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Oversight is specified as a role and a meeting. The reviewer sees a score without the underlying case, has no authority to change a policy, and no defined route when they disagree."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Report the share of oversight points with a complete specification, and the number of reviewer-initiated state changes per thousand reviews alongside median review time."

faqs:
  - question: "Why retire human in the loop?"
    answer: "Because it names a position rather than a capability. A human can be in the loop and still lack information, authority, time, or permission to disagree, which means they are not part of the control system."
  - question: "Does every automated step need one?"
    answer: "No. It is needed wherever an oversight claim is being made. If no oversight is claimed for a step, the specification is not required, but the claim cannot then be made in an assurance document."
