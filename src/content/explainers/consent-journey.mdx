---
title: "What is a consent journey?"
description: "A consent journey maps how consent is requested, renewed, revoked, and recorded over time."
permalink: "/explainers/consent-journey"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "consent-journey"
definition:
  - "A consent journey maps consent from the first request through renewal, revocation, and expiry. It treats consent as a series of choices, not a single checkbox."
  - "A well-built journey issues a receipt for each choice and logs every change, so a person can prove what they agreed to and when."
  - "Consent counts only where refusing is possible. A person who cannot get the service without agreeing has not chosen the system, and the cost of leaving it belongs in the journey map."
example: "A health data app asks for consent at sign-up and asks again every 90 days. A person can revoke it in one click and download a receipt."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Every consent state, mapped"
    detail: "List each state: requested, granted, renewed, revoked, expired. Say what moves a person from one to the next, and give them a receipt for each move."
  - title: "Asking again when the purpose changes"
    detail: "Most programs ask once and never again. Ask again when the purpose, the model, or the parties the data is shared with change."
  - title: "Proof that revocation works"
    detail: "Publish how long a revocation takes to take effect, how many renewals are completed, and the consent history an auditor can check."

faqs:
  - question: "How often should consent be renewed?"
    answer: "More often for riskier data. A sensitive data flow needs a shorter renewal window and a clear reminder."
  - question: "What makes a consent journey auditable?"
    answer: "A log that ties each change to its scope, its purpose, and the owner who approved it. A receipt alone shows what the person chose, not what the system did with the choice."
