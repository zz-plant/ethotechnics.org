title: "What is a Consent Journey?"
description: "A consent journey maps how consent is requested, renewed, revoked, and recorded over time."
permalink: "/explainers/consent-journey"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "consent-journey"
definition:
  - "A consent journey maps the full lifecycle of consent from initial request to renewal, revocation, and expiration. It treats consent as a sequence of explicit choices rather than a single checkbox."
  - "Strong consent journeys issue receipts and log every change so people can prove what they agreed to and when."
example: "A health data app asks for consent at onboarding, requires renewal every 90 days, and provides a one-click revoke option with a downloadable receipt."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Lifecycle map"
    detail: "Document each consent state (requested, granted, renewed, revoked, expired) with explicit triggers and user-visible receipts."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Most programs collect consent once and never revisit context changes; add renewal checkpoints when purpose, model, or data sharing changes."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Publish revocation latency, renewal completion rates, and screenshots of consent history so auditors can verify enforceability."

    answer: "Renewal should match risk and sensitivity; higher-risk data flows warrant shorter renewal windows and clear reminders."
  - question: "What makes a consent journey auditable?"
    answer: "Auditability requires receipts for every consent state change, plus logs that tie the change to scope, purpose, and owner."
