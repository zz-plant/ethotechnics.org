title: "What is time-to-halt (TTH)?"
description: "Time-to-halt (TTH) is the time between a request to stop an automated system and the moment it stops acting."
permalink: "/explainers/time-to-halt"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "time-to-halt"
definition:
  - "Time-to-halt (TTH) is the time from a request to stop a system to the moment it has stopped acting. The request can come from an operator or from a person the system affects. A TTH target says how fast a halt has to work."
  - "This explainer measures that one interval: halt request to stop. The casebook's “time to halt” measures a different one, from the first harm to the halt. Robodebt was halted the month the Federal Court forced the question, three years and four months after the first harm. A system can stop fast on request and still run for years before anyone with authority makes the request."
example: "A payments platform sets a five-second TTH target. Any queued transfer stops within five seconds of a person pressing the halt control."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Where the clock stops"
    detail: "The clock stops when the last downstream action has stopped. A screen saying the request was received does not stop it."
  - title: "The slowest halts"
    detail: "An average can look fine while the slowest halts still do harm. Set p95 and p99 targets for the actions that do the most harm."
  - title: "A record of each missed target"
    detail: "Keep a TTH histogram for each endpoint, the dependencies that slowed it, and a review of every missed target."

faqs:
  - question: "What is a reasonable TTH target?"
    answer: "The faster an action does harm, the shorter the target. The highest-risk actions need to stop in under a second, or within a few seconds."
  - question: "Does a fast halt fix the problem?"
    answer: "No. A halt contains the harm. The rule that produced it still has to be changed by its owner, and the people harmed still have to be restored."
