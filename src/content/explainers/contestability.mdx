---
title: "What is contestability?"
description: "Contestability is the ability to challenge an automated decision and get a review with the authority to change it."
permalink: "/explainers/contestability"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "contestability"
definition:
  - "Contestability means a person affected by an automated decision can challenge it, and the challenge can change it. It needs notice that a decision was made, a clear route to appeal, and a reviewer with the authority to change the outcome."
  - "Each step leaves a receipt, runs to a deadline, and ends in a recorded outcome."
  - "Upheld challenges also have to reach the rule. A system that reverses cases one at a time, while the rule that produced them keeps running, is contestable only in name."
example: "An eligibility denial includes an appeal button. A person reviews the appeal within 72 hours, and each step issues a receipt."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What a challengeable decision record holds"
    detail: "At least the reason code, the source of the evidence, the owner, the deadline, and who can reverse the decision."
  - title: "A reviewer who can reverse"
    detail: "An appeal link with no one behind it who can reverse the outcome is review for show. Name who can reverse, and publish the deadline."
  - title: "Reversals counted by rule"
    detail: "Report the reversal rate for each decision type and rule, the median time to remedy, and the share of appeals with complete receipts. Send each rule's count to its owner."

faqs:

    answer: "Six parts: standing to challenge, the reasons for the decision, a record of it, a deadline, a remedy, and protection from retaliation for challenging."
  - question: "Is a high reversal rate a good sign?"
    answer: "It shows the route works. It is also evidence that the rule is wrong. A rule with many upheld challenges goes back to its owner. It is not proof that the appeals process works."
