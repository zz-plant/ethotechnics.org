---
title: "What is Justified Delegation?"
description: "Justified delegation is a delegation whose authority, evidence, and correction capacity are all still in force today."
permalink: "/explainers/justified-delegation"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "justified-delegation"
definition:
  - "A justified delegation is one whose grant sits in an allowed state, whose policy records have not expired, and whose correction obligation is still resourced. Justification is a present-tense property of the delegation, not a fact about the review that approved it."
  - "Teams use it to replace the question of whether a launch was approved with the question of whether the delegation would be approved again on today's evidence."
example: "A claims triage delegation is suspended automatically when its population-drift policy record passes its review date, and it resumes only after a fresh drift study is filed and the grant is renewed."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What to check"
    detail: "For any past decision, show that the grant authorizing it was in an allowed state at the time, and that every policy record it cited was inside its validity window."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "The launch review is treated as permanent justification. Nothing expires, nothing is renewed, and the absence of complaints is read as continued evidence."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Publish the share of consequential decisions taken under a grant in an allowed state, and the median age of live grants against their renewal interval."

faqs:
  - question: "How is this different from an approved deployment?"
    answer: "Approval is a past event. Justification is a current state, and it lapses when the evidence behind the grant expires or the correction capacity behind it is withdrawn."
  - question: "What makes a delegation stop being justified?"
    answer: "An expired policy record, a grant past its renewal date, an unresourced correction obligation, or observed action outside the grant's scope."
