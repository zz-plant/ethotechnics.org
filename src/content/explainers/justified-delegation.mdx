---
title: "What is justified delegation?"
description: "Justified delegation is a delegation whose authority, evidence, and correction capacity are all still in force today."
permalink: "/explainers/justified-delegation"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "justified-delegation"
definition:
  - "A delegation is justified when three things are true today. Its grant is in the allowed state: it has not been sent for review, suspended, or revoked. The policy records it rests on have not passed their expiry dates. And the people and budget needed to correct its errors are still in place."
  - "Justification describes the delegation now, not the review that approved it. The question changes from whether the launch was approved to whether the delegation would be approved again on today's evidence."
example: "A claims triage delegation is suspended automatically when its policy record on population drift passes its review date. It resumes only after a new drift study is filed and the grant is renewed."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "A grant in force at the time"
    detail: "For any past decision, show that the grant behind it was in the allowed state at the time. Show that every policy record it cited was within its validity window: after it was adopted and before its expiry or review date."
  - title: "Launch approval treated as permanent"
    detail: "The launch review is treated as permanent justification. Nothing expires and nothing is renewed. The absence of complaints is read as continued evidence."
  - title: "Decisions made under a live grant"
    detail: "Publish the share of consequential decisions made under a grant in the allowed state, and the median age of live grants against their renewal interval."

faqs:
  - question: "How is this different from an approved deployment?"
    answer: "Approval is a past event. Justification is a current state. It lapses when the evidence behind the grant expires or the capacity to correct it is withdrawn."
  - question: "What makes a delegation stop being justified?"
    answer: "An expired policy record, a grant past its renewal date, no one resourced to correct its errors, or the system acting outside the grant's scope."
