---
title: "What are ethical interrupts?"
description: "Ethical interrupts are deliberate checkpoints that pause automation when risk thresholds are crossed."
permalink: "/explainers/ethical-interrupts"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "ethical-interrupts"
definition:
  - "An ethical interrupt is a checkpoint that pauses automation when a stated risk threshold is crossed. The system does not go on until a person reviews the case or confirms that it should."
  - "A good interrupt is visible and logged, and the case can go back the way it came. The pause has an owner and a record."
  - "An interrupt that fires again and again on the same rule is evidence about the rule, not only a case to clear. Count interrupts by rule and send the count to whoever owns the rule."
example: "A content moderation pipeline pauses a case when the model's confidence is below a threshold and the possible harm is high. The case goes to a human reviewer."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Three kinds of interrupt"
    detail: "A prompt asks a person to confirm. A hard stop blocks the action. An escalation sends the case to someone with more authority. Use the lightest one that works."
  - title: "A named trigger for each"
    detail: "If triggers are vague, interrupts become popups people click through. Tie each one to a named risk signal and a condition under which it lapses."
  - title: "Whether the interrupts were right"
    detail: "Track how often interrupts fire on cases that needed them, and what reviewers decided. Send each rule's count to its owner with the date the rule was last revised."

faqs:
  - question: "When should an ethical interrupt trigger?"
    answer: "When the system's confidence is low, the possible harm is high, or the outcome cannot be undone without a human review."
  - question: "Do ethical interrupts slow systems too much?"
    answer: "They add friction only where a risk threshold is crossed. Low-risk cases run at full speed, and high-harm cases get a human review."
