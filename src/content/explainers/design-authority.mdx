title: "What is Design Authority?"
description: "Design authority names the accountable owner who can approve, change, or halt a system’s design."
permalink: "/explainers/design-authority"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "design-authority"
definition:
  - "Design authority identifies the accountable owner empowered to approve, change, or halt a system's design. It prevents diffusion of responsibility by naming a steward with decision rights."
  - "Clear design authority ties governance decisions to a person or office that can sign off on risks and remediation."
example: "A platform names a Design Authority for its recommender system and requires that role to sign off on any changes to ranking logic or safety thresholds."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Authority boundary"
    detail: "Clarify who can approve high-impact UX patterns, who can block dark patterns, and how exceptions are escalated."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "When design authority is symbolic, risky experiments bypass review under growth exceptions; require prelaunch sign-off trails."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Store design decision records, rejected-pattern logs, and release gates showing authority exercised before deployment."

faqs:
  - question: "How is design authority different from product ownership?"
    answer: "Product ownership focuses on delivery goals, while design authority carries governance power to approve, halt, or remediate based on ethical risk."
  - question: "What happens when authority changes?"
    answer: "Update the authority record, issue a receipt for the handoff, and ensure prior decisions remain traceable to the former owner."
