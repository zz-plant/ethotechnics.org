---
title: "What is design authority?"
description: "Design authority names the accountable owner who can approve, change, or halt a system’s design."
permalink: "/explainers/design-authority"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "design-authority"
definition:
  - "Design authority is the named owner who can approve, change, or halt a system's design. Naming one person or office keeps responsibility from spreading so thin that no one holds it."
  - "When challenges against the design are upheld, the count goes to this owner. The owner changes the design or records why not."
example: "A platform names a design authority for its recommender system. That role must sign off on any change to the ranking logic or the safety thresholds."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What the authority covers"
    detail: "Say who can approve a high-impact interface pattern, who can block a deceptive one, and how exceptions are escalated."
  - title: "Sign-off before launch"
    detail: "Where the authority is symbolic, risky experiments skip review as growth exceptions. Require a record of sign-off before launch."
  - title: "A record of the authority used"
    detail: "Keep design decision records, a log of rejected patterns, and release gates that show the authority signed off before deployment."

faqs:
  - question: "How is design authority different from product ownership?"
    answer: "A product owner is responsible for delivery. A design authority can approve, halt, or require changes to a design because of the risk it poses to people."
  - question: "What happens when authority changes?"
    answer: "Update the authority record and issue a receipt for the handover. Earlier decisions stay traceable to the former owner."
