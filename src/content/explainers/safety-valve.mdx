title: "What is a Safety Valve?"
description: "Safety valves are fallback mechanisms that release pressure when automation risks harm."
permalink: "/explainers/safety-valve"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "safety-valve"
definition:
  - "A safety valve is a mechanism that releases pressure when systems are overloaded, uncertain, or error-prone. It creates a safe fallback path, often human-led, instead of allowing harmful automation to continue unchecked."
  - "Safety valves keep accountability intact during exceptions by making the fallback visible and logged."
example: "During a surge in automated appeals, the system routes high-risk cases to a human review queue instead of auto-denying to meet deadlines."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "Trigger design"
    detail: "Define concrete thresholds that activate the valve (uncertainty spikes, queue pressure, anomaly confidence) and route to human fallback."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Safety valves fail if activation waits for executive approval; automate trigger execution and log who disabled any safeguard."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Report activation frequency, fallback resolution times, and harm-prevention cases where valve intervention changed outcomes."
