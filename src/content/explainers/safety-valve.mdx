---
title: "What is a safety valve?"
description: "A safety valve routes cases to a logged fallback path, often human review, when automation is overloaded, uncertain, or error-prone."
permalink: "/explainers/safety-valve"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "safety-valve"
definition:
  - "A safety valve sends cases to a fallback when a system is overloaded, unsure, or making errors. The fallback is often a person. It stops harmful automation from running on unchecked."
  - "During an exception the fallback is visible and logged, so each case routed through it still has an owner and a record."
example: "During a surge in appeals, the system sends high-risk cases to a human review queue instead of denying them automatically to meet deadlines."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What opens the valve"
    detail: "Set the thresholds that open it, such as a jump in uncertainty, a growing queue, or an anomaly, and name the human fallback it routes to."
  - title: "No wait for approval"
    detail: "A valve that waits for an executive's approval opens too late. Open it automatically, and log anyone who switches a safeguard off."
  - title: "Cases the valve changed"
    detail: "Report how often it opens, how long fallback cases take, and the cases where the fallback changed the outcome."

faqs:
  - question: "Is a safety valve the same as an exception?"
    answer: "No. An exception is a label for a kind of case. A safety valve is the fallback that keeps people safe when such a case occurs."
  - question: "What if the valve keeps opening?"
    answer: "If it opens often under one rule, the rule is producing cases it cannot decide. The count goes to the rule's owner, who changes the rule or records why not."
