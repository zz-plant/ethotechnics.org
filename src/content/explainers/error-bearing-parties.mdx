title: "Error-Bearing Parties"
description: "The people who absorb a system's errors are the first to see failures and the last able to act, unless standing gives them a route in."
permalink: "/explainers/error-bearing-parties"
eyebrow: "Explainer"
glossaryRef: "error-bearing-party"
definition:
  - "An error-bearing party is anyone who absorbs the consequences of a system's errors: the people its decisions fall on, and the staff whose corrective labor the system depends on in order to stay usable."
  - "Naming them is the precondition for standing. Exposure that has no procedural route into the system is signal the institution has chosen not to receive."
example: "A benefits eligibility system lists two error-bearing parties per decision class: the claimant whose payment is withheld, and the caseworker who spends unrecorded hours reversing miscategorized claims. Both can raise a challenge through the same mechanism."
distinctSignals:
  - title: "What to check"
    detail: "The standing register names parties by role for each decision class, including internal operators, and each named party can reach the system's own challenge channel without escalating through a manager."
  - title: "Common failure pattern"
    detail: "Only external users are counted. The exception-handling labor that keeps the system looking reliable stays invisible, so the institution never sees the failure rate it is actually absorbing."
  - title: "Evidence to ship"
    detail: "Report the share of decision classes with at least one named error-bearing party, and the volume of corrective labor attributed rather than absorbed."

faqs:
  - question: "Why include staff as error-bearing parties?"
    answer: "Because the system depends on their corrective labor to remain usable. If that labor is uncounted, the institution is reading a reliability figure that other people are paying for."
  - question: "Does standing mean a veto?"
    answer: "No. It means a challenge must be received, answered by a named responder on a clock, and capable of producing a state change. It need not prevail."
