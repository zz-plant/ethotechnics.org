      <section id="relationship-to-std-07" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Relationship to STD-07</h2>
        <p>
          <a href="/standards/std-07-revisable-delegation-record">
            STD-07, the Revisable Delegation Record
          </a>{" "}
          defines the record format. It says how an authorization, an action, a
          discrepancy, and an objection must be written down so that a later
          reader can reconstruct what was believed and what was permitted. This
          standard governs the terms of the delegation those records describe:
          what a grant must show to keep standing, what makes the policy it
          rests on still valid, what a human reviewer must be able to do before
          the review counts as a control, and how much correction capacity the
          delegation owes.
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-08 does not restate STD-07. Where the record format already binds
          an obligation, this standard cites it. The clauses below add only the
          terms STD-07 leaves open, and an implementation of STD-08 presupposes
          STD-07 Level 2 conformance or better.
        </p>

      <section id="scope" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Scope</h2>
        <p>
          This standard applies to any delegation of consequential agency: a
          grant under which a model, an agent, a rules engine, a service, or a
          person acting on the institution's behalf may take actions that change
          someone's status, access, resources, or risk. It is
          substrate-neutral. Nothing in it depends on which component makes the
          decision, and nothing in it requires a particular control plane.
        </p>
        <p>
          The question STD-08 serves is not whether the delegate is capable. It
          is whether the delegation still deserves to stand. Four things can
          decay while the system keeps working: the justification behind the
          grant, the policy the justification rests on, the human intervention
          that was counted as a control, and the institution's capacity to
          correct what the delegate does. Each is one part below.
        </p>
        <p>
          The standard binds Laws II, III, IV, IX, and XI of the{" "}
          <a href="/standards/laws">
            Laws for Engineering Delegated Intelligence
          </a>
          . Laws V, VI, and XII are bound by{" "}
          <a href="/standards/std-06-human-impact-safety-case">STD-06</a>{" "}
          because dependence is a property of the deployment rather than of a
          single grant. Law VII is bound by{" "}
          <a href="/standards/std-02-contestability-recourse">STD-02</a>.
        </p>
      </section>

      <section id="part-a" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Part A: Authority as lease (Laws II, XI)</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A grant is a lease, not a title. Nothing about it renews itself."
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-07 §2.2 already requires a delegation to record its scope, holder,
          grantor, mode, ceiling, expiry, and revocation conditions, and holds
          that a delegation with no revocation conditions is a transfer rather
          than a delegation. What it does not say is what has to be true for the
          lease to be extended, or what happens when the scope grows. This part
          says both.
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.1</span>
            Renewal burden scales: The evidence a renewal must produce MUST rise
            with the duration the grant has already run and the consequence of
            the actions it authorizes. The grant states, in
            <code>renewal_basis</code>, what that evidence is before the review
            period begins, so the bar cannot be set after the result is known.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.2</span>
            Silence is not renewal: A renewal MUST NOT rest solely on the
            absence of observed failure. The renewal record names what was
            examined, what would have been visible if the grant had been
            failing, and who looked. A grant renewed on an unexamined quiet
            period is recorded as unrenewed and moves to
            <code>review_required</code>.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.3</span>
            Expansion is a new authorization: Widening a grant's scope,
            population, action classes, or ceiling MUST be taken as its own
            authorization decision, with its own <code>evidence_basis</code> and
            its own correction-capacity check under §4.2. It is not a
            continuation of the existing authorization and it does not inherit
            the earlier decision's evidence.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.4</span>
            Expansion is recorded as a transition: Every widening MUST appear in
            <code>state_history</code> as a transition whose reason is
            <code>expansion</code>, with the date, the deciding authority, and a
            reference to the evidence produced under §1.3. Scope that grew
            without such an entry is treated as ungranted scope.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section id="part-b" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Part B: Policy validity (Law III)</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Policy is a state of the system, not an input it consumed once."
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-07 §3.1 and §3.2 already require a record to say what it rests on
          and what would invalidate it. A grant that names its policies in
          <code>depends_on</code> satisfies that. This part addresses what those
          policies must themselves be, and what a fired trigger does to the
          grants downstream of it.
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.1</span>
            Policy is a record: A policy a grant relies on MUST itself be a
            record carrying <code>provenance</code>, <code>assumptions</code>,
            <code>review_triggers</code>, <code>expires_at</code>, and
            <code>status</code>. A threshold, model card claim, or eligibility
            rule held only in configuration or in a deck is not a policy record
            and cannot be cited as the basis of a grant.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.2</span>
            Grants name their policies: A grant MUST list in
            <code>policy_refs</code> every policy record its
            <code>evidence_basis</code> depends on, pinned to a
            <code>version</code>. An unpinned reference is a reference to
            whatever the policy has since become.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.3</span>
            A fired trigger changes status: When a condition in
            <code>review_triggers</code> is met, the policy MUST move to
            <code>review_required</code> within the trigger's declared clock.
            Recording the observation without the status change does not
            satisfy this clause.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.4</span>
            Dependent grants follow: Every grant whose
            <code>policy_refs</code> include a policy in
            <code>review_required</code> or <code>suspended</code> MUST move to
            <code>review_required</code> within the same clock, and a
            reconsideration is opened against each. The grant may be confirmed
            and returned to <code>allowed</code>; it may not stay in
            <code>allowed</code> without that decision being taken.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.5</span>
            Expiry ends justification: A policy past its
            <code>expires_at</code> MUST NOT justify a live grant. Either the
            policy is renewed as a new version with fresh provenance, or the
            grants resting on it lose their basis and move to
            <code>review_required</code>. Expiry is not a warning state.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section id="part-c" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Part C: Intervention (Law IX)</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A human who can only approve is not in the loop. They are beside it."
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-07 §2.2 records whether a delegation runs unattended, on confirm,
          or forbidden. That is the mode. It says nothing about whether the
          confirming human can see the problem, prevent the action, alter the
          state, or survive disagreeing. This part is the gap the record format
          leaves, and it is where the phrase "a human reviews" stops being an
          answer.
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.1</span>
            Every oversight claim resolves to a specification: Any requirement,
            control description, or assurance claim asserting that a human
            reviews, approves, supervises, or monitors a delegated action MUST
            resolve to an intervention specification naming
            <code>owner</code>, <code>information_available</code>,
            <code>actions_preventable</code>, <code>states_alterable</code>,
            <code>on_disagreement</code>, <code>incentives</code>,
            <code>cost_to_exercise</code>, and <code>reach_time_target</code>.
            An unresolved oversight claim is a finding.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.2</span>
            Confirm mode is specified: Every grant whose <code>mode</code> is
            <code>confirm</code>, the mode STD-07 §2.2 requires the
            authorization to record, MUST carry
            <code>intervention_ref</code> pointing at that specification. A
            confirm mode with no specification behind it is recorded as
            unattended, because that is how it behaves.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.3</span>
            Approval without state change is not a control: If
            <code>states_alterable</code> is empty, or the actions the owner can
            prevent are not the actions the delegation takes, the arrangement
            MUST NOT be recorded as a control, cited in a safety case, or
            counted toward a correction-capacity claim under §4.1. It may be
            recorded as advisory review, under that name.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.4</span>
            Intervention is measured: The institution MUST measure, per
            specification and per review period, the approval rate, the median
            and tail time spent per approval, and the share of approvals
            reaching the owner inside <code>reach_time_target</code>. An
            unmeasured specification is a claim, not a control.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.5</span>
            Near-unanimous approval is a finding: Sustained approval rates at or
            near unanimity, or a median approval time too short for the
            information named in <code>information_available</code> to have been
            read, MUST open a reconsideration of the specification. The finding
            is not that the reviewer is at fault. It is that the control has not
            been shown to be one.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section id="part-d" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Part D: Correction symmetry (Law IV)</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Delegating the authority to act is delegating the capacity to be
          wrong at scale."
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-07 §4.2 obliges an answer to an accepted objection within its
          clock. That is one component of correction and this standard relies on
          it. What is left open is the proportionality: how much correction
          capacity a delegation of a given size owes, and what happens to that
          ratio when the delegation grows.
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.1</span>
            Correction capacity is stated: A grant MUST state its correction
            capacity across seven components: detection, that a wrong action can
            be noticed; challenge, that it can be raised; standing, that someone
            with exposure may raise it; review, that it will be examined by a
            named party within a clock; authority to modify, that the examiner
            can change the grant or the decision; reversible state transitions,
            that the affected state can be returned; and practical ability to
            operate after correction, that the institution can absorb the
            reversal. Each component names its evidence.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.2</span>
            Expansion requires matching capacity: Authority MUST NOT be expanded
            under §1.3 without a matching expansion of correction capacity,
            evidenced at the same decision. Where capacity cannot be shown to
            have grown with scope, the expansion is refused or the scope is
            narrowed to what the existing capacity covers.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.3</span>
            Capacity is reviewed with scope: Correction capacity MUST be
            re-examined at every renewal under §1.1, against the volume and
            consequence of what the grant actually did in the period rather than
            what it was expected to do. Capacity that has degraded moves the
            grant to <code>review_required</code>.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.4</span>
            Practical ability is evidenced, not asserted: The seventh component
            of §4.1 SHOULD be evidenced from the deployment's dependency record
            under
            <a href="/standards/std-06-human-impact-safety-case">STD-06</a>,
            using <code>correction_latency</code> and the institutional level of
            <code>reversibility</code>. A correction the institution cannot
            afford to make is not counted as capacity.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>
