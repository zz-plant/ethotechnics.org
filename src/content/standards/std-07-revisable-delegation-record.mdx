      <section id="scope" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Scope</h2>
        <p>
          An institution that delegates consequential work to machines usually
          has sensing, memory, analytics, policy, and execution. What it lacks
          is one record connecting them, so it can know something has changed
          and keep doing exactly what it was doing. This standard defines that
          record: one append-only shape for what the institution believed, what
          it could do, what it had authorized, what it did, where reality
          diverged, who objected, and what it revised.
        </p>
        <p>
          Ethotechnics is the engineering of delegated power such that
          exercising it does not destroy the material conditions of its own
          revision. The question this standard serves is how an institution
          can become more capable without becoming less revisable. The
          problem is recursive: an institution delegates because a machine
          system is useful, reorganizes around it as it succeeds, and so
          alters the conditions under which the delegation was made. The
          record exists so the institution can still change its mind inside
          that process.
        </p>

        <RevisableDelegationRecursion />

        <p>
          STD-07 does not say which beliefs are right, which capabilities to
          build, or which delegations to grant. It says how each of those must
          be written down so a later reader, human or machine, can answer four
          questions about any moment: what was believed at the time, what was
          authorized, what happened, and what was learned. It is a record
          format, in the sense of STD-04 and STD-05, and it is meant to be
          emitted by systems that already keep their own logs. Adopting it
          means mapping an existing log onto this shape, not replacing it.
        </p>
        <p>
          STD-07 sits beside this site's existing
          <a href="/standards/decision-record.schema.json">Decision Record</a>
          schema rather than replacing it. A Decision Record is the full
          receipt for one automated decision affecting a person: owner, clocks,
          reason codes, evidence links. It maps onto an STD-07
          <code>action</code> record whose <code>depends_on</code> and
          <code>authority</code> say what justified it. STD-07 adds the other
          seven kinds, so the decision can be read against the beliefs,
          capabilities, and authorizations that were in force when it was made.
        </p>
        <p>
          <a href="/standards/std-08-delegation">STD-08, Delegation</a> sits
          above this one. STD-07 defines the record format; STD-08 governs the
          terms of the delegation the record describes: how renewal burden
          scales, when a widening of scope needs its own authorization, and
          what makes a <code>confirm</code> mode meaningful. An implementation
          of STD-08 presupposes conformance with STD-07, and STD-08 does not
          restate what the record format already binds.
        </p>
        <p>
          The standard applies to any system whose outputs change what an
          institution does: a forecast registry, a capability graph, an
          operating brief, a change monitor, an approval gate. It is
          domain-neutral by construction.
        </p>
      </section>

      <section id="premises" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Premises</h2>
        <p>
          Six commitments the record shape is built to hold, each with the
          field that carries it.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>
            A system can become more capable without becoming more
            authorized. <code>capability</code> and
            <code>authorization</code> are separate kinds; one never implies
            the other.
          </li>
          <li>
            Authority expires when its evidentiary basis weakens.
            <code>authorization.depends_on</code> names that basis, and
            <code>invalidated_by</code> names what would end it.
          </li>
          <li>
            Evidence triggers reconsideration rather than merely producing
            telemetry. A <code>discrepancy</code> that matches an invalidation
            condition owes a <code>revision</code> within a clock.
          </li>
          <li>
            People who absorb a system's failures have a route through which
            their observations acquire institutional force.
            <code>contest.standing</code> and <code>objection</code> records.
          </li>
          <li>
            Successful automation increases scrutiny of dependence rather
            than relaxing it. <code>depends_on</code> is populated on the
            records that rest on others, so dependence is visible before it
            is irreversible.
          </li>
          <li>
            No system may consume the expertise, alternatives, or evidence
            needed to question and replace it. Records are append-only and
            hashed, so the account of what was believed and authorized
            survives the system that produced it.
          </li>
        </ul>
      </section>

        <h2>The loop</h2>
        <p>
          A record's <code>kind</code> names which step of the institution's
          cognitive loop it belongs to. Each kind answers one question.
        </p>

        <p>
          The loop runs perceive → believe → know what can be done → decide
          what authority is justified → act → detect mismatch → revise. No
          single system owns the whole loop. One system emits beliefs, another
          capabilities and authorizations, another actions, another
          discrepancies. The record shape is what lets them refer to one
          another: an action names its authorization, an authorization names
          the beliefs it rests on, a revision names the discrepancy that forced
          it.
        </p>

      <section id="articles" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article I: Time and immutability</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A record that can be edited is not a record of what was believed at
          the time."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.1</span>
            Two clocks: Every record carries <code>time.as_of</code>, the moment
            in the world it describes, and <code>time.recorded_at</code>, when
            it was written. They are never collapsed, so a contemporaneous
            record can be told from a reconstruction. A belief that will later
            be scored also carries <code>time.available_at</code>, when its
            evidence became available to the actor.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.2</span>
            Append-only: No record is edited or deleted. A change is a new
            record whose <code>supersedes</code> names the old one. The old
            record remains readable. A correction is visible forever, which is
            the point.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.3</span>
            Declared expiry: A record whose validity has a horizon declares it
            in <code>time.valid_until</code>. A record past its expiry is stale,
            not wrong; it needs a renewal or a revision, and a consumer treats
            it as neither current nor superseded.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article II: Authority</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Describing authority is not mediating it. The record must show which
          authorization each action ran under."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.1</span>
            Action under authorization: Every <code>action</code> record names
            the <code>authorization</code> record it ran under in
            <code>authority.authorization_record</code>. An action with no
            authorization is a finding, not a record.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.2</span>
            Bounded delegation: Every <code>authorization</code> record names
            its scope, holder, grantor, mode (unattended, confirm, or
            forbidden), ceiling where one exists, expiry where one exists, and
            the conditions under which it is revoked. A delegation with no
            revocation conditions is a transfer, not a delegation.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.3</span>
            Delegation chain: Where the actor acts for a principal,
            <code>actor.on_behalf_of</code> names that principal. The chain of
            <code>on_behalf_of</code> links, read back to a human or an
            institution, is the answer to "who authorized this".
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.4</span>
            Clause citation: A record produced to satisfy a published
            obligation cites it in <code>authority.clauses</code>, pinned by
            version in the emitting system's manifest.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article III: Dependence and invalidation</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A record that does not say what would change its mind cannot be
          revised, only replaced."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.1</span>
            Dependence: <code>belief</code>, <code>authorization</code>, and
            <code>action</code> records list in <code>depends_on</code> the
            records they rest on. An authorization depends on the beliefs and
            capabilities that justified it; an action depends on its
            authorization.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.2</span>
            Invalidation conditions: Those same records list in
            <code>invalidated_by</code> the checkable conditions which, if they
            became true, would mean the record no longer holds. Each condition
            may name where it is checked and a clock.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.3</span>
            Discrepancy handling: When a <code>discrepancy</code> record matches
            an invalidation condition on a live record, the emitting system
            produces a <code>revision</code> or an <code>objection</code>
            within the condition's clock. Silence past the clock is a
            governance failure, not a pending state.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§3.4</span>
            Capability gating: A <code>capability</code> record distinguishes
            <em>configured</em> from <em>verified</em>. An authorization that
            depends on a capability is valid only while that capability's
            latest record is verified.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article IV: Standing and contest</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Who gets to object is part of the record, not a policy about it."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.1</span>
            Standing declared: A record that affects people outside the
            emitting system declares in <code>contest.standing</code> who may
            object, in plain language, and in <code>contest.channel</code>
            where an objection is accepted.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.2</span>
            Objection answered: An <code>objection</code> record accepted on
            the declared channel produces a <code>revision</code> or a reasoned
            refusal, itself a record, within <code>contest.reversal_clock</code>.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.3</span>
            Visibility named: Every record carries <code>visibility</code>. A
            public record is citable; an internal record is auditable inside
            the institution; a private record is tenant data and leaves the
            institution only with consent.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article V: Integrity</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Git history is tamper-evident for the people who read git."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.1</span>
            Canonical hash: Every record carries
            <code>integrity.hash</code>, the SHA-256 of its canonical
            serialization: the record without its integrity block, keys sorted
            recursively, no insignificant whitespace, UTF-8. Two emitters
            serializing the same record produce the same hash.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.2</span>
            Chained stream: Records in one system's stream carry
            <code>integrity.prior_hash</code>, the hash of the record before
            them, so a missing or reordered record is detectable.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.3</span>
            Stable identifiers: <code>record_id</code> is never reused, even
            after supersession. The recommended form is
            <code>&lt;system&gt;:&lt;kind&gt;:&lt;local-id&gt;</code>.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section id="adoption" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Adoption notes</h2>
        <p>
          STD-07 was drafted against five systems that each hold a fragment of
          the loop and did not share a record shape: a forecast registry with a
          ledger hash and three clocks, a capability graph with signed
          approvals and an authority table, a weekly operating brief with an
          append-only decision memory and assumption snapshots, a change
          monitor with Merkle-verified event bundles, and this framework's own
          clause vocabulary. None of them conformed when this draft was
          written. Each is expected to declare a conformance level in its
          machine-readable manifest and raise it as the mapping lands, starting
          from Level 0.
        </p>
        <p>
          Two of them now emit the shape. The weekly operating brief exports
          its decision memory as <code>belief</code> records at Level 1,
          mapping the assumption snapshot it already kept onto
          <code>invalidated_by</code>. The capability graph emits
          <code>capability</code>, <code>authorization</code>,
          <code>discrepancy</code> and <code>revision</code> at Level 2, and
          its gate now narrows an unattended grant whose prerequisite has
          started failing — enforced at the decision, independently of whether
          the record was ever written, which is the relationship between
          Article III and a running system that this standard is trying to
          describe.
        </p>
        <p>
          Until this draft nothing read either stream, and a record format with
          emitters and no readers is a documentation format. The{" "}
          <a href="/diagnostics/record-conformance">
            Record Conformance Checker
          </a>{" "}
          is the reader: it grades a stream against the level its emitter
          declares, so a claim can be contradicted by something other than good
          faith. No emitting system consumes another's records yet, which is a
          different and still-open gap.
        </p>
        <p>
          Three rules for adopters. First, map the existing log; do not build a
          second one. A system that already writes an assumption snapshot has
          <code>invalidated_by</code>; it needs to say so in this shape.
        </p>
        <p>
          Second, declare what is not there. A system whose environment adapter
          is a fixture emits no <code>action</code> records, because an action
          record from it would attest to something that did not happen in the
          world; it says so beside the level it claims rather than leaving a
          reader to assume otherwise.
        </p>
        <p>
          Third, validate against a consumer rather than against yourself. The
          first system to emit Level 2 records had every field of every record
          matching this schema and was rejected by the validator on all four,
          because its store wrote <code>2026-09-06 20:35:26</code> where
          <code>time.as_of</code> requires an ISO 8601 instant. A serializer
          that is correct about structure and wrong about a format is the
          defect this shape exists to surface, and it is not findable from
          inside the system that produced it.
        </p>
      </section>
