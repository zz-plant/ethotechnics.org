<SectionBlock
  id="overview"
  eyebrow="Overview"
  title="What it is"
  description="A management-system standard for establishing and improving AI governance policies and processes."
>
  <p>
    ISO/IEC 42001 specifies how organizations set up, implement, and maintain
    an AI management system, including roles, policies, objectives, and continual
    improvement cycles.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="critique"
  eyebrow="Ethotechnics critique"
  title="Where the standard falls short"
  variant="alt"
>
  <ul class="card__list">
    <li>
      <strong>Governance effort ≠ governability:</strong> Audits can certify documented
      controls while the system remains hard to stop mid-incident.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Continuous improvement can normalize slow harm:</strong> “We’re improving”
      can coexist with multi-quarter remediation cycles in high-stakes contexts.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Artifacts over interruption capacity:</strong> Documentation, training, and
      lifecycle governance are easier to evidence than tested rollback authority.
    </li>
  </ul>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="failure-mode"
  eyebrow="Failure mode"
  title="Certified responsibility + unresolved time-in-harm"
  description="Well-managed organizations can still leave users stuck in harm windows."
>
  <p>
    The failure mode is a system that passes audits while users still cannot
    compel reversal on a clock.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>
