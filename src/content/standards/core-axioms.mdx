<SectionBlock
  id="axioms"
  eyebrow="Axioms"
  title="The axioms of accountable systems"
  description="Use these as a doctrinal checklist when authoring or auditing standards."
>
  <ol class="axiom-list">
    <li>
      <strong>Axiom 1 — Finitude:</strong> Human attention is finite and must be
      treated as a protected resource.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Axiom 2 — Consent:</strong> Legitimate systems secure informed
      consent at every critical handoff.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Axiom 3 — Stewardship:</strong> Operators accept full
      responsibility for the outcomes and latency of their automation.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Axiom 4 — Reversibility:</strong> High-impact actions require safe
      rollback paths and transparent appeal channels.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Axiom 5 — Legibility:</strong> Users must be able to see,
      understand, and audit the burden imposed on them.
    </li>
  </ol>
</SectionBlock>


<SectionBlock
  id="derivation"
  eyebrow="Derivation"
  title="Why these are owed, not recommended"
  description="The axioms are stated above as doctrine. This is the argument beneath them, which decides whether an operator may treat them as good practice to be traded off."
  variant="alt"
>
  <p>
    A model evaluation establishes properties of a model. It cannot establish
    the alignment of the system that model is deployed into, because that system
    includes the people who notice what the model got wrong, interpret what it
    left ambiguous, and absorb what it broke. Where those corrections are
    routine rather than exceptional, the people making them are not users of the
    safety architecture. They are part of it.
  </p>
  <p>
    That produces a specific asymmetry, and it is the reason these axioms are
    entitlements rather than preferences. An institution that depends on a
    population's corrective capacity while withholding corrective authority has
    delegated a safety function without delegating the power to exercise it. It
    asks for judgment and refuses the standing that judgment implies.
  </p>
  <p>
    The claim is narrower than a general argument for participation, and the
    narrowness is what makes it hold. Ordinary productive labor makes a system
    valuable; extracting it without a say is a distributive wrong. Corrective
    labor does something further:{" "}
    <a href="/glossary/extraction-by-endurance">absorbing a failure competently</a>{" "}
    removes the evidence that it occurred. The metric improves <em>because</em>
    someone caught it. So the institution loses not only the labor but its own
    capacity to know how the system is performing — a{" "}
    <a href="/glossary/fail-silent">fail-silent</a> state in which the absorbing
    humans are the mechanism. Dependence on correction is therefore
    self-concealing in a way dependence on production is not, and a clean
    dashboard cannot be read as evidence of health until absorption has been
    measured separately.
  </p>
  <p>
    Each axiom follows from that. <strong>Finitude</strong> holds because a
    system that treats attention as unbounded is budgeting for absorption it has
    not counted. <strong>Stewardship</strong> holds because responsibility that
    is delegated downward while authority stays upward is not stewardship at
    all. <strong>Legibility</strong> holds because burden that cannot be seen
    cannot be governed, and corrective burden is the kind that hides by default.
    <strong>Reversibility</strong> and <strong>Consent</strong> hold because a
    correction that cannot be exercised is a correction only in name.
  </p>
  <p>
    This bounds the claim as well as grounding it. Authority follows demonstrated
    dependence, so it is proportional rather than unlimited, and it needs an
    instrument: the{" "}
    <a href="/evals/burden-concealment">Burden Concealment evals</a> measure how
    far reported performance depends on absorption that is not being counted.
    It also has a failure mode worth naming, because standing that tracks
    <em>current</em> indispensability rewards staying indispensable. Standing
    should attach to dependence that has been documented, and survive the
    dependence being engineered away — otherwise the doctrine preserves the
    fragility it exists to expose.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>
