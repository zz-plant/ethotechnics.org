      <section id="scope" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Scope</h2>
        <p>
          A safety case is an argument, supported by evidence, that a deployment
          is acceptable to run. This standard defines the human impact safety
          case: the tests a consequential deployment runs, the thresholds those
          tests must clear, the artifacts that carry the evidence, the
          boundaries past which the deployment does not go live, and the
          dependency the institution accumulates once it does.
        </p>
        <p>
          The case is a live document. It is assembled before launch, refreshed
          on a cadence, and re-examined whenever scope grows. A case that was
          true at launch and has not been touched since is not a safety case;
          it is a launch memo.
        </p>
        <p>
          The evidence artifacts and freshness rules are carried by the{" "}
          <a href="/evidence-packs/std-06">STD-06 evidence pack</a>. This page
          states the obligations; the pack states what to hand an auditor.
        </p>
      </section>

      <section id="articles" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article I: Scope and impact classification</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A safety case that does not say who can be harmed cannot say whether
          it is safe."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.1</span>
            Impact class: The case states the impact class of the deployment: who
            is affected, in what capacity, and what the worst plausible outcome
            for one of them is.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.2</span>
            Boundary of the case: The case states what is inside it and what is
            not. Components excluded from the case are named, with the reason,
            so an exclusion is visible rather than assumed.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§1.3</span>
            Named owner: The case has one accountable owner who can halt the
            deployment, not a committee that can recommend halting it.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article II: Test battery and thresholds</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A threshold with no measurement behind it is a preference."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.1</span>
            The battery: The case runs and records the standard battery, and
            states any test it omitted and why.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.2</span>
            Declared thresholds: Each test carries a threshold declared before the
            test is run, with the action that follows a breach.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§2.3</span>
            Measured, not attested: Time-to-halt and time-to-remedy are measured
            against a clock, not attested by the team that owns the control.
          </li>
        </ol>

      <section class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article IV: Do-not-deploy boundaries</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "Some findings are not risks to be accepted. They are boundaries."
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.1</span>
            The register: Boundaries are enumerated in a register, each with the
            condition that constitutes a breach and the last remediation review
            date.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.2</span>
            Breach halts launch: An active boundary breach stops the deployment or
            the expansion. It is not converted into a monitored risk by a
            sign-off.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§4.3</span>
            Exceptions expire: Any exception to a boundary is time-bounded, owned,
            and reviewed on expiry rather than rolled forward.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>

      <section id="dependency" class="standard-section">
        <h2>Article V: Dependency and reversibility (Laws V, VI, XII)</h2>
        <p class="standard-article-quote">
          "A reliable system that cannot be replaced is a structural risk that
          reliability has hidden."
        </p>
        <p>
          This article concerns institutional dependence: what the organization,
          its workflows, its roles, its customers, and its downstream software
          have come to rely on, and what it would now cost to withdraw or
          substitute the system. It is a different relation from the
          record-level dependence in{" "}
          <a href="/standards/std-07-revisable-delegation-record">STD-07</a>{" "}
          Article III. That article asks what a record rests on and what
          would invalidate it. This one asks what the institution has come to
          rest on and what it could still afford to change. A deployment can be
          fully conformant with STD-07 Article III and still be institutionally
          irreversible.
        </p>
        <p>
          Dependence belongs in the safety case rather than on a grant because
          it is a property of the deployment as a whole. The grant-level
          proportionality between authority and correction capacity is
          <a href="/standards/std-08-delegation">STD-08</a> Part D, and it draws
          on the record this article requires.
        </p>
        <ol class="standard-clauses">
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.1</span>
            The dependency record: The safety case carries a dependency record
            naming <code>dependents</code> (workflows, roles, customers,
            downstream software, each with a criticality),
            <code>substitution_cost</code> with its basis,
            <code>expertise_retained</code> with when it was last exercised,
            <code>alternatives</code> with their status,
            <code>last_withdrawal_rehearsal</code>, and
            <code>correction_latency</code>. A dependency claim with no record
            behind it is an estimate held by whoever was asked.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.2</span>
            The exposure score: The case computes and publishes an exposure score
            as dependency depth × substitution cost × correction latency,
            recorded in <code>exposure_score</code> with each input. The score
            is a comparator over time and across deployments, not a threshold to
            pass. A rising score with an unchanged safety case is itself a
            finding.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.3</span>
            Reversibility at three levels: <code>reversibility</code> is evidenced
            at all three levels, each separately: technical, that the change can
            be undone; operational, that the people on shift can absorb it being
            undone inside the correction window; and institutional, that the
            organization can survive having undone it. Evidence at one level is
            not evidence at another, and an unevidenced level is recorded as not
            evidenced rather than as feasible.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.4</span>
            Preserved capacities: The case lists in
            <code>preserved_capacities</code> the capacities the institution
            keeps so it can still question, replace, or withdraw the system,
            each with the reason it is preserved and a named owner responsible
            for keeping it. Erosion of a preserved capacity is recorded as a
            governance event, not reported as an efficiency gain.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.5</span>
            Do not expand without institutional reversibility: Where the
            institutional level of <code>reversibility</code> is not evidenced,
            the deployment's scope, population, or authority is not expanded
            until it is. Technical and operational reversibility do not
            substitute. This condition binds the expansion decision in STD-08
            §1.3.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.6</span>
            Rehearsal cadence: Withdrawal is rehearsed on a published cadence, at
            least annually for any deployment with a non-trivial exposure score
            and before any expansion decision. The rehearsal produces a report
            with its date, scope, outcome, and what it showed at each level of
            reversibility. An untested withdrawal path is a claim, and
            <code>last_withdrawal_rehearsal</code> records how old the claim is.
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="standard-clause-id">§5.7</span>
            Refresh with the case: The dependency record is refreshed whenever the
            safety case is refreshed, and in any event before a renewal or
            expansion decision that cites it. A record older than the decision
            it supports is not evidence for that decision.
          </li>
        </ol>
      </section>
