Institutions are in the business of demanding that human beings adapt. A patient must learn the patient portal. A clinician must master the alert routing queue. A welfare claimant must compile three years of bank statements. A worker must absorb the erratic schedule generated by an algorithmic optimization engine.

Most governance frameworks ask whether these demands are legally authorized, procedurally uniform, or technically efficient. Almost none ask the foundational question:

> Under what conditions, if any, may an institution legitimately consume another person's finite capacity to cope?

When an arrangement answers that question by silence, it treats human adaptive capacity as an infinite, uncosted resource. That silence is the origin of the [human crumple zone](/glossary/crumple-zone).

## Adaptive capacity as a finite social resource

Human beings do not possess infinite resilience. The capacities required to navigate institutional friction—sustained attention, working memory, emotional regulation, patience, off-hours paperwork, vigilance against automated errors, and relational care—are metabolically and temporally scarce. Every hour an individual spends deciphering a contradictory bureaucratic notice or manually translating data between two incompatible software systems is an hour subtracted from sleep, caregiving, creative craft, and ordinary life.

In the language of political economy, adaptive capacity is the ultimate input of [social reproduction](/research/theory/what-ethotechnics-is-not): the background labor that keeps living creatures functional from one day to the next.

When an institution designs a system that leaks errors, it does not eliminate those errors; it routes them downward. Frontline clinicians stay late to reconcile missing lab reports. Desperate parents spend days on hold attempting to correct automated benefit terminations. Because this compensatory labor occurs outside the institution's official telemetry, the institution's dashboard reports smooth operation and lean overhead. But the cost has not disappeared: it has been externalized into the bodies and lives of the people least equipped to refuse it.

## The adaptation externality

The accounting error has a name: the **adaptation externality**.

In [performance decomposition](/glossary/performance-decomposition), observed performance (`P_obs`) separates into intrinsic designed capacity (`C_des`) and human compensation (`H_comp`):

```
P_obs = C_des + H_comp
```

When an institutional balance sheet measures only `P_obs` against its own financial operating costs, it counts the output of human sacrifice while omitting the debit. It achieves high throughput by running on uncompensated human subsidy.

This is the failure mode of [counterfeit buffering](/glossary/counterfeit-buffering): human beings are nominally retained in the loop not to exercise autonomous craft, but to function as liability sponges and error-signal attenuators. Their heroic efforts keep the system from crashing, which paradoxically manufactures the evidence that the design is sound. By absorbing the impact, they blind the institution to the necessity of its own reform.

## Genuine versus manufactured necessity

Not all friction is illegitimate. Living in a material world with mortal bodies, limited energy, and complex coordination requires real effort. The crucial task is distinguishing **genuine necessity** from **manufactured necessity**.

| Dimension                  | Genuine necessity                                                                                 | Manufactured necessity                                                                                          |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Origin**                 | Irreducible physical scarcity, mortality, natural complexity, open-ended collaboration.           | Unreconciled databases, rigid software schemas, departmental silos, budget caps on intake staff.                |
| **Alternative**            | No feasible institutional redesign can eliminate the friction without violating physical reality. | Friction could be eliminated by reallocating the institution's internal budget or automating internal handoffs. |
| **Burden distribution**    | Shared collectively or absorbed through reciprocal, recognized care.                              | Asymmetric: concentrated downward on the claimant, patient, or frontline worker.                                |
| **Institutional response** | Transparent acknowledgment; continuous effort to buffer the vulnerable.                           | Defensive denial; treating the friction as "fraud prevention" or the subject's personal incompetence.           |

**Genuine necessity** is the friction of the world. It is the complex clinical reality of a patient with five interacting chronic illnesses; it is the physical difficulty of repairing a power grid after a storm. Meeting genuine necessity calls for discretionary **craft stewardship**—the skilled, uncoerced human engagement with open-ended reality.

**Manufactured necessity** is the friction of uncorrected institutional architecture. It is the requirement that an applicant re-verify their identity four times across three agencies that refuse to share records. It is the nurse having to click through seventy irrelevant pop-up alerts before administering medication. It persists because for the institution, transferring the burden of coping onto participants is cheaper than fixing the upstream rule.

When an institution justifies manufactured necessity by calling it unavoidable, it commits a category error: it mistakes the limits of its own budget for the laws of nature.

## The emancipatory criterion

Understanding adaptive capacity as a scarce resource changes the criterion of institutional progress.

Technocratic reform typically focuses on **expansion of coping capability**: teaching claimants how to navigate convoluted portals, training clinicians in "resilience" and mindfulness, or deploying consumer-facing AI agents to automate the filing of appeals.

While coping tools may provide temporary relief, treating them as progress is perverse. When institutions discover that people have become better at coping, they routinely raise the baseline demand. Better consumer tools are met with more complex administrative obstacles, raising the minimum stamina required to secure basic rights.

The emancipatory criterion of Ethotechnics is therefore the opposite:

> Genuine progress is not expanding individual capacity to endure bad systems; it is the **systemic reduction of compulsory dependence on exceptional capability**.

An arrangement is not just because a hero can navigate it. It is just only when an exhausted, distracted, non-expert person with ordinary human limitations can live inside it with dignity, without having their life consumed by the process of survival.

## Architectural constraints: the binding laws

The principle connects to three of the laws:

### 1. The correction obligation (Law IV)

Every delegated automated action creates an institutional obligation to correct its errors. If an automated decision generates a false rejection, the burden of discovering, documenting, and contesting that mistake cannot be externalized onto the affected subject. The time and adaptive capacity consumed by the correction process must be tracked as an operational debit against the system's authorization grant.

### 2. Proportional standing for error-bearing parties (Law VII)

Whoever absorbs the adaptation externality must hold [corrective standing](/glossary/corrective-standing) capable of forcing upstream rule revision. If an institution relies on frontline nurses or benefit recipients to patch its defects, those actors cannot be confined to advisory committees or customer feedback surveys. They must possess the formal standing to introduce counterevidence that invalidates the operating model's assumptions.

### 3. Preserving the conditions of contestability (Law XII)

No system may consume the very resources required to contest it. An appeal process that requires thirty hours of legal research and paperwork from an unhoused person violates Law XII: it wins by attrition, using exhaustion to manufacture acquiescence. A legitimate system must ensure that the friction of contestation is lower than the friction of submission.

## Conclusion: the right to unconsumed life

To ask what an institution may demand is ultimately to ask what human life is for.

Human beings were not born to serve as shock absorbers for inflexible databases. They were not created to spend their finite days translating between bureaucratic categories that cannot perceive them.

The goal of ethotechnical design is not a friction-free society, nor is it a fully automated world where machines replace human relation. It is the sober, rigorous construction of institutions that respect the metabolic reality of the creatures living inside them: systems that absorb their own errors, account for their own burdens, and return to ordinary people the one thing no balance sheet can restore: their own time.
