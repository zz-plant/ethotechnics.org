Ethotechnics asks one question of an automated decision system: when it is wrong, does evidence of the harm reach someone who has to change it? It tracks the answer through six safeguards: capability, authority, evidence, dependency, standing, and correction. It does not say what a good institution is. The boundary matters because a discipline that means everything means nothing. The companion essays describe what it builds: an [engineering tradition](/research/theory/an-engineering-tradition) for delegated agency, and the design stance of [ethotechnical design](/research/theory/ethotechnical-design).

## The layer it occupies

Ethotechnics sits between principles and institutions. The political anthropology, that people are finite, dependent, varied, and adaptive, is not Ethotechnics. Nor are the principles that follow from it, such as that the burden of adapting should not fall on one side, out of sight, until it destroys capacity. Ethotechnics is the layer that asks how an arrangement can be made to behave as the principles require.

```
political anthropology → normative principles → ethotechnics → mechanisms → lived consequences
```

It does not study what institutions ought to value. It studies how a value becomes a property of an arrangement. Corrigibility as a norm says an institution should be open to revision. Corrigibility as a capacity answers the questions that follow. How does the institution notice its categories failing? How do affected people make a failure legible? Who receives the objection, and what standing does the objector hold? Who can revise the rule? What makes admitting error possible? How long does correction take, and does the institution remember what it learned?

Agreeing that an institution should be corrigible says little about what to build. [Law VI](/standards/laws#law-vi) keeps the norm and the capacity apart: nominal reversibility is not operational reversibility. The same gap separates every other virtue from its mechanism. Ethotechnics works on the mechanism.

## Prevention and correction

The discipline has two sides. The corrective side builds institutions that detect and repair mismatch. The protective side designs institutions so that they do not demand unnecessary adaptation in the first place. Prevention asks whether a form, rule, category, interface, or workplace can accommodate predictable human variation by default. Correction asks whether, when a remainder appears anyway, the people who meet it can make the arrangement change.

The two are not alternatives to trade. A system that can be corrected only if its citizens spend fifteen hours a week governing it has solved one problem by creating another. If people must become experts in an institution to survive it, or must keep reporting errors, appealing classifications, and organizing to make it behave reasonably, participation has become one more adaptation burden. The design problem is not to maximize participation. It is:

> Maximize effective corrective power while minimizing the labor required to exercise it.

The best corrective institution may be one people rarely think about, because it learns on the occasions they do correct it. A correction mechanism that uses up the [capacity it is meant to protect](/glossary/preserved-capacity) has failed in a way its own metrics will not show.

## The parts

Across unrelated domains, including medicine, software, bureaucracy, employment, and AI, a few mechanisms recur. Reversibility matters everywhere. Escalation matters whenever frontline staff find problems they cannot fix. Exception capacity matters whenever a standard system meets varied cases. Collective representation matters when individual complaints are too weak to move an organization. Institutional memory matters because correction without retention is endless rediscovery.

These are where political commitments acquire causal force. A right to challenge a decision is close to worthless if using it costs forty hours, specialist knowledge, repeated paperwork, and months without benefits. The test of an appeal is not whether it exists. It is whether the arrangement turns a mismatch someone experiences into a legible claim, standing, and a revised rule.

The glossary carries most of the parts under their own names: [contestability](/glossary/contestability), [reversibility](/glossary/reversibility), [escalation](/glossary/escalation-horizon), [corrective standing](/glossary/corrective-standing), [feedback proximity](/glossary/feedback-proximity), [preserved capacity](/glossary/preserved-capacity), and [institutional learning](/glossary/institutional-learning). The open question is empirical, and more tractable than finding the ideal institution: **which combinations of these parts produce correctable institutions, and under which conditions?**

## What it is not

The boundary is easiest to state as a list of retirements. The words need not disappear from the writing. Each stops explaining anything on its own and becomes a question the harder terms have to answer.

**Ethotechnics is not the technology of producing good people.** That phrase points at shaping subjects, and it carries a disciplinary weight the argument does not need. The question is how arrangements distribute demands for adaptation, and whether they preserve or consume human capacity.

**Ethotechnics is not humane design.** Ergonomic interfaces, pleasant bureaucracies, paternalistic nudges, and personalized AI can all count as humane while authority stays insulated. The argument applies harder tests: burden, standing, correction, reversibility, jurisdiction, and capacity.

**Ethotechnics is not friction reduction.** [Friction can protect](/research/theory/friction-as-accidental-governance): waiting periods, procedural hurdles on authorities, documentation requirements for coercive decisions. The question is where friction sits and who has to overcome it, not how little of it remains.

**Ethotechnics is not user-centeredness.** User-centered design can improve a person's interaction with a system while leaving the system's jurisdiction untouched. It can make an illegitimate demand convenient. The prior question is whether this institution should be demanding this from the person at all. [The model of a person](/research/theory/model-of-a-person) develops that question through four roles, which show why serving the user can still be hostile to the subject.

**Ethotechnics is not resilience.** The ability to persevere is not an obligation to compensate indefinitely. No institution can remove all difficulty, so human resilience is indispensable. Because it is indispensable, there are limits on what an institution may demand of it. The resilience of persons is a different thing from the correctability of arrangements. Observed performance has two parts:

```
observed institutional performance = designed capacity + human compensation
```

When the institution leaves the second term out of its accounts, its success is overstated. The compensation also hides the failure: people who patch errors at their own cost keep the signal from reaching any alarm. The method separates [intrinsic performance](/glossary/intrinsic-performance), what the formal system achieves, from [compensated performance](/glossary/compensated-performance), what people's absorption covers. It holds to the [principle of non-expropriation of resilience](/glossary/principle-of-non-expropriation-of-resilience): a person's capacity to adapt does not give the institution a claim on that adaptation.

**Ethotechnics is not organizational viability.** An institution can stay productive, safe, and adaptable because its people and their families absorb costs that never appear in its measures. Keeping the institution running and improving the lives of the people who run it are different objectives. A reform that makes people's sacrifices more sustainable may be worth doing. It is not a reform that makes the institution depend on them less, and it should be reported as what it is.

**Ethotechnics is not empowerment.** Information, dashboards, choices, training, and complaint channels are routinely called empowering while nobody gains the ability to cause reconsideration, reversal, or a rule change. Without [corrective standing](/glossary/corrective-standing) backed by real capacity, what is called empowerment is better coping.

**Ethotechnics is not voice.** Institutions can become skilled at hearing without changing. Surveys, consultations, feedback forms, and public comment can increase expression without moving any corrective power. Voice matters only after [standing, transmission, and revision](/research/theory/insulation) are in place.

**Ethotechnics is not participation maximization.** Participation spends finite human capacity. An institution that requires constant vigilance has moved its governance costs onto its citizens.

**Ethotechnics is not adaptability, and not flexibility.** Who is adapting, to what, at whose initiative, at whose cost, and with what alternative? An adaptable workforce may be one forced to compensate for rigid management. One actor's flexibility is often produced by another's permanent availability. The object of study is how flexibility and rigidity are distributed, not how much there is of either.

Two more words need narrowing rather than retirement. **Accommodation** leaves the rule intact and makes an exception for the person who cannot conform. That is sometimes right. But repeated accommodations raise the question of why the rule has not changed, and accommodation can become the way correction is avoided. Following the disability-justice critique of compulsory adaptation, Ethotechnics treats the need for exceptional coping as evidence of a defect in the environment, not in the person. **Care** can compensate for terrible institutions. An ethic of care without an analysis of adaptation sanctifies the [human crumple zone](/glossary/extraction-by-endurance), the person who absorbs the failure so the system runs smoothly. Feminist social reproduction theory documents how unpriced care work subsidizes formal economic output. Sometimes the achievement is not more caring people but an arrangement that needs less heroic care.

## The questions that replace the vocabulary

The last retirement is the founding ambition. Ethotechnics does not specify the good institution. It assumes that no institutional form removes mismatch, no designer anticipates every remainder, no procedure guarantees justice, and no criterion captures the whole human good. So it does not ask how to design the institution correctly. It asks whether the institution's errors become somebody else's permanent burden.

The older picture ran from good intentions through good design to good outcomes. The replacement runs from mismatch to retained learning:

```
inevitable mismatch → visible burden → corrective standing → institutional adaptation → retained learning
```

Five questions do the work the retired words used to claim: **who adapts, who pays, who can correct, what changes when correction succeeds, and how much human capacity the arrangement consumes in the meantime.**

## What the laws take from this

The five questions become checkable through five laws.

- [Law IV](/standards/laws#law-iv): every consequential delegation creates a correction obligation. Correction capacity is a stated quantity the institution is measured against, not an intention.
- [Law VI](/standards/laws#law-vi): nominal reversibility is not operational reversibility. This is the gap between a norm and its mechanism, written as a test.
- [Law VII](/standards/laws#law-vii): error-bearing parties require standing proportional to exposure. It answers "who can correct" with a procedure: a challenge is received, answered, and able to change state.
- [Law VIII](/standards/laws#law-viii): observability without state transition is theater. That is voice without revision, named.
- [Law XII](/standards/laws#law-xii): no system may erase the conditions of its own contestability. It is the protective side as a constraint: a correction mechanism may not use up the capacity it exists to protect.

None of the five uses a retired word. Each asks what changes, and for whom, when the system is wrong.
