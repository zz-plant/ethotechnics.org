This essay is the discipline's account of its own boundary. The companion essays say what Ethotechnics is: an [engineering tradition](/research/theory/an-engineering-tradition) for delegated agency, the practice of building powerful systems that remain answerable to the people they cannot fully represent, and the positive design stance of [ethotechnical design](/research/theory/ethotechnical-design). This essay says what it is not, because the name has at times been asked to carry more than the argument can support, and a discipline that means everything means nothing.

## The layer it occupies

Ethotechnics sits at a specific level. The political anthropology — that people are finite, dependent, heterogeneous, and adaptive — is not Ethotechnics. The normative principles that follow from it — that adaptation burdens should not become systematically one-sided, insulated, or capacity-destroying — are not Ethotechnics either. Ethotechnics is the layer between the principles and the institutions: the discipline that asks how arrangements can be made to behave in the way the principles require.

The sequence runs:

```
political anthropology → normative principles → ethotechnics → mechanisms → lived consequences
```

Ethotechnics does not study what institutions ought to value. It studies how values become properties of arrangements. That is why it is a design discipline rather than a theory of the good, and why the rest of this essay is a series of refusals rather than a program.

## Values become properties

The difference between the discipline and the theory is visible in one substitution. Corrigibility as a norm is the statement that an institution should be open to revision. Corrigibility as an engineered capacity is the answer to the questions that follow: how does an institution notice its categories failing, how do affected people make failures legible, who receives the objection, what standing they hold, who has revision authority, what makes acknowledgment of error possible, how quickly correction happens, and whether the institution remembers what it learned.

Agreeing that an institution should be corrigible tells us surprisingly little about what to build. The norm and the capacity are not the same object, and [Law VI](/standards/laws#law-vi) is the standing refusal to treat them as one: nominal reversibility is not operational reversibility. The same distance separates every other virtue from its mechanism. Ethotechnics concerns the mechanism.

## Two sides

Because the discipline is about the relationship between arrangements and human agency, it has two faces rather than one.

The **corrective** side builds institutions capable of detecting and repairing mismatch. The **protective** side designs institutions so that they do not generate unnecessary demands for human adaptation in the first place. Ethotechnics is the sum:

```
mismatch prevention + mismatch correction
```

Prevention asks whether the form, rule, category, interface, workplace, or bureaucracy can accommodate predictable human variation by default. Correction asks whether, when the inevitable remainder appears anyway, the people encountering it can cause the arrangement to change. A system that is wonderfully corrigible provided its citizens spend fifteen hours a week governing it has solved one problem by creating another, which is why the two sides are not alternatives to be traded. They are the same arrangement seen from two directions.

## Primitives

Across otherwise unrelated domains — medicine, software, bureaucracy, employment, AI — a small set of mechanisms recurs. Reversibility matters everywhere. Escalation matters whenever frontline actors discover problems they cannot themselves fix. Exception capacity matters whenever standardized systems meet heterogeneous cases. Collective representation matters when individual complaints are too weak to overcome organizational incentives. Institutional memory matters because correction without retention is endless rediscovery.

These are not administrative details appended to political philosophy. They are where political commitments acquire causal force. A nominal right to challenge a decision is almost worthless if exercising it costs forty hours, specialized knowledge, repeated documentation, and months without benefits. The test of an appeal is not whether it exists but whether the arrangement reliably converts experienced mismatch into a legible claim, effective standing, and institutional revision.

The glossary already carries most of the parts under their own names — [contestability](/glossary/contestability), [reversibility](/glossary/reversibility), [escalation](/glossary/escalation-horizon), [corrective standing](/glossary/corrective-standing), [feedback proximity](/glossary/feedback-proximity), [preserved capacity](/glossary/preserved-capacity), [institutional learning](/glossary/institutional-learning). What remains open is the empirical question, which is more tractable than discovering the ideal institution: **which combinations of these produce corrigible institutions under which conditions?**

## The objective

Framing the discipline this way also keeps it from collapsing into proceduralism — from corrigibility quietly meaning "give people more voice." The broader argument says that is not enough. If people must become experts in an institution merely to survive it, something has gone wrong. If they must constantly report errors, appeal classifications, monitor administrators, and organize collectively to force institutions to behave reasonably, then participation itself has become another adaptation burden.

The design problem is therefore not to maximize participation. It is:

> Maximize effective corrective power while minimizing the labor required to exercise it.

The best corrective institution may be one people rarely have to think about, because it learns efficiently on the occasions when they do need to correct it. That is the link to [preserved capacity](/glossary/preserved-capacity): a correction mechanism that consumes the very capacity it is supposed to protect has failed in a way its own metrics will not show.

## What it is not

The boundary is easiest to state as a list of retirements. None of these words needs to disappear from the writing. Each stops doing explanatory work on its own and becomes something that has to be answered through the harder questions.

**Ethotechnics is not the technology of producing good people.** That formulation points at subject formation and carries a disciplinary weight the argument does not need. The question is not how institutions produce ethical subjects but how arrangements distribute demands for adaptation and preserve or consume human capacities.

**Ethotechnics is not humane design.** Ergonomic interfaces, pleasant bureaucracies, paternalistic nudges, and personalized AI can all qualify as humane while leaving authority insulated. The argument supplies harder tests: burden, standing, correction, reversibility, jurisdiction, capacity.

**Ethotechnics is not friction reduction.** [Friction can protect](/research/theory/friction-as-accidental-governance): waiting periods, procedural hurdles on authorities, documentation requirements for coercive decisions. The relevant question is where friction should reside and who should have to overcome it, not how little of it remains.

**Ethotechnics is not user-centeredness.** User-centered design can optimize a person's interaction with a system while leaving the system's jurisdiction untouched, making an illegitimate demand wonderfully convenient. The prior question is whether this institution should be demanding this from the person at all — the question [the model of a person](/research/theory/model-of-a-person) develops through its four roles, which show why optimizing for the user can still be hostile to the subject.

**Ethotechnics is not resilience.** Resilience can mean people becoming extraordinarily capable of absorbing institutional failure, which is the phenomenon the framework exists to expose. Distinguish the resilience of persons from the correctability of arrangements; often the significant fact is that human resilience has allowed an incorrigible institution to survive — the point behind the [falsified denominator](/glossary/falsified-denominator) and the [workaround presumption](/glossary/workaround-presumption).

**Ethotechnics is not empowerment.** Information, dashboards, choices, training, and complaint channels are routinely described as empowering while leaving unchanged whether anyone can cause reconsideration, reversal, or rule revision. Without [corrective standing](/glossary/corrective-standing) backed by effective capacity, empowerment is improved coping.

**Ethotechnics is not voice.** Institutions can become sophisticated at hearing without changing: surveys, consultations, feedback forms, and public comment can increase expression without redistributing corrective power. Voice matters only downstream of [standing, transmission, and revision](/research/theory/insulation).

**Ethotechnics is not participation maximization.** Participation consumes finite human capacity, and an institution that requires constant vigilance privatizes its governance costs onto citizens. The target is low-cost effective correction.

**Ethotechnics is not adaptability, and not flexibility.** Who is adapting, to what, at whose initiative, at whose cost, with what alternative? A remarkably adaptable workforce may simply be one forced to compensate for managerial rigidity. One actor's flexibility is often produced by another's permanent availability; the relevant object is the distribution of flexibility and rigidity, not the quantity of either.

Two further words need narrowing rather than retirement. **Accommodation** leaves the rule intact and creates an exception for the person who cannot conform; that is sometimes right, but repeated accommodations raise the question of why the rule has not changed, and accommodation can become the mechanism by which correction is prevented. **Care** can compensate for terrible institutions, and an ethic of care without an analysis of adaptation sanctifies the [human crumple zone](/glossary/extraction-by-endurance). Sometimes the achievement is not more caring people but an arrangement that requires less heroic care.

## The five questions

The deepest retirement is the founding ambition itself. Ethotechnics does not specify the good institution. It assumes that no institutional form eliminates mismatch, no designer anticipates every remainder, no procedure guarantees justice, and no criterion captures the whole human good. It therefore does not ask how to design the institution correctly. It asks:

> How do we construct institutions whose inevitable incorrectness does not become somebody else's permanent burden?

The older picture ran from good intentions through good design to good outcomes. The replacement runs from inevitable mismatch through visible burden and corrective standing to institutional adaptation and retained learning:

```
inevitable mismatch → visible burden → corrective standing → institutional adaptation → retained learning
```

That is the anti-utopian core, and it is what the retirements purchase. Five questions now do more theoretical work than the vocabulary they replaced: **who adapts, who pays, who can correct, what changes when correction succeeds, and how much human capacity does the arrangement consume in the meantime?** The compact form of the discipline follows from them:

> Ethotechnics is the engineering of conditions under which power can be corrected without requiring people to exhaust themselves correcting it.

## What the laws take from this

The method does not require a reader to accept this scoping to adopt its constraints. It takes five, each already bound elsewhere.

From Law IV it takes that every consequential delegation creates a correction obligation, which is why the discipline is about correction capacity rather than good intentions. From Law VI it takes that nominal reversibility is not operational reversibility, the refusal that separates the norm from the mechanism. From Law VII it takes that error-bearing parties require standing proportional to exposure, the answer to "who can correct." From Law VIII it takes that observability without state transition is theater, the precise description of voice without revision. And from Law XII it takes that no system may erase the conditions of its own contestability, which is why the protective side — the arrangement that does not consume the capacities it exists to protect — is not an optional adjunct but the other half of the same discipline.
