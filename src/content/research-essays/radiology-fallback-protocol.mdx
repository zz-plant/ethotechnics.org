<SectionBlock
  id="question"
  eyebrow="Open question"
  title="What can people still do after years of automation?"
  description="A draft protocol, not a study. No data have been collected."
>
  <p>
    A system can improve routine performance and still leave the people who use
    it less able to read a case unaided, less able to correct a single output,
    or less able to change the workflow when the system is wrong. Those are
    three different losses, and a study that measures only the first will miss
    the other two.
  </p>
  <p>
    The study asks whether starting with AI-assisted practice changes what a
    reader can do independently at week eight. A separate drill records who can
    halt or reverse a decision under the site's procedures. This separates a
    reader's skill from their authority to intervene.
  </p>
  <p>
    This protocol extends the{" "}
    <a href="/research/scholarly-crossings#oversight-decay-test">
      oversight decay test
    </a>
    into one clinical task. It is not a test of whether AI is good or bad in
    radiology.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="measures"
  eyebrow="Measures"
  title="What would be measured"
  description="Three outcomes, kept separate because they can move in different directions."
  variant="alt"
>
  <ul>
    <li>
      <strong>Task performance.</strong> Accuracy and reliability on
      expert-adjudicated chest-radiograph cases, scored against a reference
      standard fixed before data collection.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Governability.</strong> Whether a reader can challenge a
      suggestion and halt or reverse a decision, and how long that takes. A
      sandbox drill records who can execute a pause and who can request a rule
      change.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Long-term effects.</strong> Independent clinical judgment on
      blinded, unaided reads; the availability of a practiced fallback; and the
      authority to change the workflow. The first is measured at baseline and
      week eight. The second and third are recorded from the site’s procedures
      and the sandbox drill.
    </li>
  </ul>
  <p>
    The three are reported separately. A gain in accuracy does not offset a loss
    in the ability to halt a decision, and a loss in unaided accuracy does not
    by itself show a loss of authority.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="design"
  eyebrow="Design"
  title="One offline chest-radiograph task"
  description="Readers complete both practice blocks in a randomly assigned order."
>
  <p>
    One group starts with AI-assisted practice and then practices unaided. The
    other group completes the same blocks in reverse. The blocks use the same
    number of cases, the same feedback, and equal time. Both groups spend the
    same total time practicing with AI.
  </p>
  <p>
    Cases are chest radiographs with a reference standard agreed by two
    radiologists blinded to the AI suggestion; disagreements go to a third.
    Readers complete a baseline block of unaided reads, the two practice blocks,
    and a week-eight block of unaided reads. The baseline and week-eight reads
    use cases held out from practice, and no AI suggestions are shown.
  </p>
  <p>
    A separate arm tests responses to suggestions that are correct and
    suggestions that are deliberately wrong. Readers are told before the task
    that some suggestions may be wrong. The wrong-suggestion arm is disclosed,
    not deceptive, and every participant is debriefed.
  </p>
  <p>
    A sandbox drill after the reading task records who can initiate a pause, who
    can execute it, who can reverse a decision, and who can request a change to
    the workflow. The drill is observed, not simulated on paper.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="outcomes"
  eyebrow="Analysis"
  title="Outcomes and analysis"
  description="Fixed before data collection; no result is reported without them."
  variant="alt"
>
  <p>
    The primary outcome is the between-order difference in change from baseline
    to week eight on blinded, unaided reads of expert-adjudicated cases.
    Secondary outcomes are accuracy and reliability with AI available, the rate
    at which readers follow correct and wrong suggestions, the time to halt or
    reverse a decision, and the sandbox record of who can change the workflow.
    The randomized comparison estimates the effect of practice order after both
    groups have completed both blocks. The sandbox drill describes which roles
    can intervene under the site's procedures.
  </p>
  <p>
    Before data collection, the registration fixes the material effect (the
    smallest change in unaided accuracy that would matter), the minimum feasible
    sample, the power calculation behind it, the stop rules, and the analysis.
    The draft does not set those numbers; it fixes the outcomes and the
    inference rules, and the study does not start until the rest are registered.
  </p>
  <p>
    Stop rules cover a prespecified decline in unaided accuracy, a prespecified
    rate of following wrong suggestions, and any sandbox finding that a reader
    cannot halt a decision the protocol says should be haltable.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>

<SectionBlock
  id="change"
  eyebrow="Inference"
  title="What would change the claim"
  description="Three results, three different conclusions."
>
  <ul>
    <li>
      An effect confined to wrong suggestions favors immediate anchoring: the
      reader follows the suggestion in front of them, and the loss is in
      correction rather than in underlying skill.
    </li>
    <li>
      A precise estimate that rules out a meaningful decline in unaided accuracy
      narrows the claim for this task. It would not show that AI-first practice
      is safe across radiology, and it would not settle authority.
    </li>
    <li>
      An imprecise estimate leaves the question open. It is not evidence of
      absence, and it is not a reason to widen the claim.
    </li>
  </ul>
  <p>
    The sandbox drill can reveal that a reader cannot halt a decision, even when
    assisted reads are more accurate. It records who can intervene at the time
    of the drill.
  </p>
</SectionBlock>
